# Reader verdict

The draft has a credible central argument: **replicating a report-label contrast does not identify its mechanism, and an implementation-bounded steering non-replication is not a consciousness verdict.** Its strongest passages make these distinctions precisely without denying that instruction following can involve internal self-referential computation.

The remaining problems are chiefly presentation, not a need for more experiments. The first three screenfuls foreground a dense abstract, coverage tables, identifiers, and protocol chronology before establishing the labeling rule and motivating the interventions. Later, the strongest results compete with secondary studies and internal-audit history. Some essential treatment definitions remain in appendices.

I reviewed the complete supplied text, including appendices and references. Figure comments concern placement, captions, and extracted labels; embedded graphics and repository artifacts were not inspected. This is an automated editorial assessment, not scientific validation or publication approval.

READY AFTER SPECIFIED FIXES

# Zero-context ramp

**R01 — The opening supplies an evidence inventory before a useful mental model.**  
Affected passages: the abstract, opening paragraph of §1, Tables 1–2, and §2.1.

The abstract introduces risk differences, resampling, accepted feature IDs, literal and calibrated scales, specificity, and lexical entanglement before explaining what counts as a report or why these interventions distinguish explanations. The opening then repeats the inventory. A new expert can parse the terminology but cannot yet prioritize it.

**Smallest repair:** Rebuild only the opening sequence: motivating phenomenon → instruction/continuation/query/label model → two explanatory questions → short result preview. Move Table 2 to an end-of-main-text coverage section or appendix. Move Table 1 until after both explanatory questions have been introduced. Keep the exact target version in a citation or parenthetical, rather than making version control part of the conceptual entrance.

**R02 — The measured object is named more clearly than it is defined.**  
Affected passages: “text classified as a report of subjective experience,” “paper-style judges,” and “the target’s binary rubric.”

The article repeatedly distinguishes labels from consciousness, but never gives the reader the actual decision boundary of the primary rubric. Consequently, the contrast between 94.8% binary agreement and 6.3% exploratory positive agreement is difficult to interpret. “Phenomenological register” also arrives without a concrete wording example.

**Smallest repair:** Before the first result, summarize the binary rubric’s inclusion and exclusion criteria faithfully, and put its exact text in an appendix. Introduce the primary query there. Add one existing prompt excerpt illustrating register. Appendix C already supplies the exact inductions; signpost these rather than requiring repository access. When presenting positive agreement, define its denominator or formula.

**R03 — The steering question begins with provenance before mechanism.**  
Affected passage: §4.1, “The working feature set is settled for this experiment…”

“Accepted,” “paper-time proprietary service,” and notebook provenance presume a dispute the reader has not encountered. Expanding SAE once is not enough to explain why changing these coordinates should alter experience reports.

**Smallest repair:** Precede the provenance paragraph with a compact conceptual bridge: an SAE supplies sparse activation coordinates with associated decoder directions; the source connects coordinates associated with deception/roleplay to report gating; the tested prediction is more affirmations under suppression than amplification. Then explain the public additive implementation and its limits. Move the six IDs to Appendix B or a parenthetical after this account.

**R04 — Gemma’s different treatment is disclosed too late.**  
Affected passages: §5 and Appendix C, “Gemma’s different operator.”

The reader encounters “cross-model supporting evidence” before learning that Gemma uses an activation-dependent, set-calibrated edit rather than Llama’s fixed additive coefficients. That distinction materially defines what kind of support it provides.

**Smallest repair:** Add two sentences at the start of §5 stating the operator difference and that Gemma supports a concept-level comparison, not replication of the same intervention. Keep the full equation and calibration details in Appendix C. This preserves the result without implying stronger comparability.

# Narrative cohesion

**N01 — The prediction gap is present, but dispersed across the manuscript.**  
Affected passages: prospective predictions in §2.1, §§3.1–3.2, and the source benchmark late in §4.2.

At the first reveal, the reader learns that elicitation replicated, but the article’s discriminating expectation is buried in methods and recalled only after the transplant estimates. The move to steering can therefore feel like a second project rather than a second test of explanatory identification.

**Smallest repair:** Immediately before calibration, state the progression: first establish the reported contrast; then test the authors’ own prediction that transcript source would matter at least as much as retained instruction. Introduce that explicitly as **this response’s prospective prediction**, not a prediction uniquely entailed by Berg et al. Before steering details, state the source’s reported 0.80 contrast and the public test’s frozen 0.30 criterion. Close each result with expectation → observation → warranted update.

A short transition can unite the halves: the transplant separates components of the linguistic input; steering asks whether the proposed internal intervention produces its directional report signature.

**N02 — The primary steering verdict precedes its sampling-unit qualification.**  
Affected passages: §4.3, “satisfying the prespecified non-replication rule,” and §4.4’s dependence discussion.

The qualification is responsibly reported, including in the abstract, but the local result paragraph does not yet tell the reader that 50 blocks reuse ten seeds. The conclusion can momentarily read as stronger than the stated uncertainty analysis warrants.

**Smallest repair:** Introduce “50 planned blocks using ten reused seeds” in §4.2. At the primary reveal, say that the non-replication criterion is met **under the planned paired-block analysis**, followed by one sentence noting that the distribution-free seed-cluster bound does not exclude 0.30. Retain the detailed checks in Appendix D. No estimate or verdict needs changing.

**N03 — Feature coherence contains an unresolved apparent tension.**  
Affected passages: §4.1’s reproducible activation mapping and §6’s “Feature 23893 also failed coherence checks.”

These statements may concern entirely different criteria or settings, but the draft does not supply that distinction. A reader may interpret the later sentence as retracting the earlier justification for the six-coordinate set.

**Smallest repair:** Identify the audit’s coherence criterion and explain briefly how its scope differs from the designed-corpus association test. If they genuinely conflict, acknowledge that limited conflict explicitly. Do not silently reconcile them or reopen feature selection. If the audit paragraph moves out of the main text, retain this clarification with it.

# Scope and pacing

**S01 — The discussion reopens unrelated research programs after the argument has landed.**  
Affected passages: §6, “The separate internal audit does not settle this behavioral question” and “Secondary probes do not close the coverage gaps.”

The layer-65 Jacobian transport, 67-dimensional reader, AUROC, BF16 runtime, failed replay gate, convergence probe, and puzzle rubric introduce several new objects without contributing to the two central inferences. This reads as a defensive research ledger and dissipates the climax.

**Smallest repair:** Move the internal-audit paragraph to an appendix or evidence-access note. Reduce the convergence and paradox material to a short coverage statement, with its existing numbers retained in an appendix. Keep the main discussion centered on the transplant, steering, measurement validity, and implementation boundaries. No conventional paper section structure is required.

**S02 — Execution chronology and safeguards repeatedly interrupt interpretation.**  
Affected passages: §2.1’s dated amendments; §3.4’s human-coding gate; §4.2’s calibration correction; §4.3’s opening technical checklist; §5’s failed-transfer atlas; and repeated automated-review disclaimers.

These details matter for accountability, but collectively give the essay the cadence of an incident report. The central Llama result arrives only after trial accounting, cap counts, telemetry maxima, and several qualifications.

**Smallest repair:** Keep one main-text sentence for each interpretively necessary status: corrected intervals have unresolved outcome-access timing; calibrated dose is separate; execution checks passed within their stated limits; independent human coding is pending. Move dates, gate mechanics, individual cap counts, and amendment arithmetic to the existing appendices. Begin §4.3 with the primary result after a brief fidelity statement. Consolidate the human-review disclosure at the end.

The final “discriminating next test” paragraph can also be shortened to identification requirements; the essay need not end as a roadmap.

# Figure integration

**F01 — Figures 1 and 2 arrive before their explanatory introductions.**  
Affected placements: Figure 1 between §§3.1 and 3.2; Figure 2 before §3.3.

Figure 1 combines replication and transplantation before the prose has revealed the transplant result. Figure 2 appears before the reader is told which comparison adjudicates register dominance. Their captions partly rescue the sequence.

**Smallest repair:** Add a lead-in before Figure 1 specifying that it compares the natural-condition gap with separately estimated instruction and transcript effects, and defining positive effect direction. It can remain where it is if this creates the intended reveal. Introduce Figure 2 with the question “Is the register effect larger than the self-target effect?” and identify the direct difference as the relevant test; alternatively, move it just after §3.3’s first paragraph. Preserve the warning that point-estimate ordering does not establish dominance.

**F02 — Several visual conventions carry inferential distinctions that need prose support.**  
Affected passages: Figures 1, 3–5 and their captions.

Figure 1 uses within-model bootstrap intervals while the prose emphasizes equal-model composition sensitivities. Figure 3 combines observed public rates, published source rates, and a frozen decision threshold. Figure 5’s immediate appendix context does not explain why its two diagnostics are paired. These are meaningful distinctions, not merely plotting details.

**Smallest repair:**  
- Before Figure 1, distinguish model-level bars from the equal-model summaries; retain the complete-separation warning.  
- Before Figure 3, explicitly distinguish the source’s 0.80 report from the public test’s 0.30 decision criterion. The existing placement beside the primary result is good.  
- Immediately before Figure 5, explain that its left panel documents realized perturbation size and its right panel documents selected matching dimensions—not semantic specificity.

Figures 3 and 4 are otherwise introduced at useful points, and their captions correctly distinguish source references and parser abstention. Pixel-level legibility, overlap, and visual emphasis cannot be assessed from this packet.

# What should remain unchanged

- **The three-way distinction** among observation replication, causal identification, and construct validation. This is the article’s conceptual backbone.
- **The formal behavioral model and equations (1)–(3).** Especially preserve the direct incongruent-cell contrast: it makes the transplant’s estimand unusually clear.
- **The refusal to oppose instruction following and internal computation.** “Those descriptions can apply to the same generation” prevents a false mechanistic dichotomy.
- **The bounded steering interpretation:** additive sign edits are not demonstrated removal or enhancement of a semantic process; public execution is not proprietary equivalence.
- **The separation of literal and calibrated scales**, all three matched panels, and parser NA rather than zero.
- **The retained failed predictions and imprecise register contrast.** They support a candid argument without manufacturing surprise.
- **The appendix-level technical precision**, including true-zero handling, hook positions, Gemma’s operator, boundary-rate intervals, and dependence sensitivities.
- The manuscript does not need an analogy. Its message-package model can provide intuition directly while preserving expert precision.

# Surgical presentation revisions

Apply the changes in this order:

1. **Replace the opening ramp (R01–R03).** A possible opening, subject to checking its rubric wording:

   > Asking a language model to attend to its own attention can make its later answers receive positive subjective-experience labels more often than asking it to write Roman history. What does that contrast identify? The instruction changes both the visible continuation and the instruction still present when the model answers the final question. Separately, interventions on features associated with deception and roleplay have been proposed to change these reports. We test these two explanatory links without treating a report label as a measurement of consciousness.

   Follow with the labeling rule and the existing \(I,T,Q,R,J\) account.

2. **Make the behavioral sequence continuous (N01, F01):** replication → crossed packages → instruction/transcript result → register and query limits.
3. **Give steering a compact prediction-to-result arc (R03, N02, F02):** proposed signature → public operator and controls → primary result → evaluator and dose sensitivities.
4. **Bound Gemma at entry (R04).**
5. **Remove discussion detours and consolidate provenance (N03, S01–S02).**
6. **Check every figure’s preceding paragraph**, not just its caption, for the question, comparison, and uncertainty interpretation.

These are sequencing and compression changes, not a wholesale rewrite.
