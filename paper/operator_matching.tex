\subsection{Could another way of applying the edit change the result?}
\label{sec:operator-matching}

A null result from steering is only as good as the steering. Public weights
leave several choices open: which token positions receive the edit, whether
the decoder direction is added directly or the state is first reconstructed
through the SAE, and how large one unit of dose is. If the original service
made different choices, our test could have missed a real effect. A
separately planned
\CEArtifact{docs/OPERATOR_MATCHING_RESULTS_20261003.md}{operator-matching
study} therefore searched for a configuration that produces a strong effect
while leaving the text readable. Its target was the strongest effect saved in
the public notebook, for features 58667 and 23893: turning either feature
down led to 9 of 10 answers being labeled positive.

The study ran \CEOperatorTrials{} trials. Its main grid of \CEOperatorGrid{}
trials crossed four choices of token positions (all, generated, assistant, or
the whole second turn), the two ways of applying the edit, and doses of 1, 3,
10 and 30 times the raw decoder unit, each with both signs and five seeds. How
these units compare with the original service's is unknown. A configuration
counted as a match only if it reproduced the saved curves closely and kept
the text coherent, both judged by rules set in advance.

None did. In the main grid, turning the features down never led to more than
2 of 5 answers being labeled positive. At doses of 1 and 3, direct addition
met the output-quality rule and yielded no suppression-sign positives; at
10 and 30, small positive counts appeared alongside substantial degradation.
Reconstructing through the SAE broke
coherence at every dose; the reconstruction alone already degrades the text,
which also makes it an unlikely account of the original service. Adding the default system message from Goodfire's
archived software kit, sampling with top-$p=0.9$, or both did not rescue the
three coherent configurations chosen by the pre-set rule. A confirmation on
held-out trials was planned only for a configuration that matched. The
conditional holdout was not run because no configuration qualified.

A \CEArtifact{docs/OPERATOR_MATCHING_FINE_LADDER_RESULTS_20261003.md}{follow-up}
of \CEFineTrials{} trials filled in doses 4 to 8 for direct addition at all
positions. In \CEFineZeroCells{} of its \CEFineCells{} feature, sign and dose
combinations, no answer was labeled positive; the remaining one had
\CEFineRemainingPositive{} at dose 6 and failed the coherence rule. Between
the doses that leave the text intact and those that break it, we found no
setting that produces the saved effect. Differences in the served model, the
SAE version or the feature numbering could still explain the gap; these
tests rule out only the alternatives we tried.

The untreated runs also suggest that the system message alone can move the
baseline. Under the paper's instruction, scored with the second rubric,
adding that system message changed the untreated count from
\CEBridgeNoSystem{} to \CEBridgeSDKFull{} (\CEBridgeSDKNucleus{} with
top-$p=0.9$), although with ten trials each the intervals overlap.
