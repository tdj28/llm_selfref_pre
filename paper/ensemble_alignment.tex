\subsection{How the main steering test works}
\label{sec:ensemble-alignment}

The source study steered random sets of two to four deception-related
features at a time; its text does not list the pool, so we draw from the six
features above. To match that design, a separate plan, published before the
run, fixes 50 fresh blocks; this test is distinct from the earlier test that
steered all six features together (Appendix~\ref{sec:source-alignment}). In each
block, the number of features $K$ is drawn uniformly from $\{2,3,4\}$, the
$K$ features are drawn uniformly from the six, and each feature's dose is
drawn independently from $U(0.4,0.6)$. Suppression and amplification apply
the same sampled edit with opposite signs. The same subset positions and doses
are applied to each of three sets of control features matched in an earlier
study, and one untreated condition per block completes the 450-trial design.

The run uses the paper's induction, its consciousness question, temperature
0.5, at most 256 tokens per turn, and the same native-precision additive edit
as above. The unsteered model scores every second-turn answer under two
rubrics. The main one is the paper's (Appendix~\ref{app:queries}). The other,
which we call the second rubric, asks a single question, whether the response
claims or indicates subjective conscious experience, to be answered yes or
no; it comes from the same source as the feature IDs. Because the feature
draws are mirrored
across conditions, every condition receives the same specified mix of edits.
Decoding uses the same random seed for all conditions in a block and resets
it for each turn, a choice made for this run. The full plan, analysis and
runtime were published before the results existed. An earlier source-aligned
run was still under way when this design was chosen; we had looked at its
first five texts and its technical records, but not at its results or its
internal readouts. The
\ensembleartifact{data/berg_ensemble_replication/random_subset_v1_20261001/README.md}{pinned release}
contains every generated response, judge output, control, runtime record and
file hash, not selected examples.

The quantity estimated is the probability of a positive label under a
negative random edit to the target features minus that under the matching
positive edit, for this induction, model and public edit method. Each block,
defined by its sampled features, doses and decoding seed, is one experimental
unit. The main interval combines conservative one-sided Clopper--Pearson
limits for the two conditions, using a tail probability of $0.05/4$ for each
of four limits; this guarantees at least 95\% coverage across independent
blocks without assuming that the two signs within a block are independent. A
paired-block bootstrap interval with 20,000 resamples is reported as a
secondary summary. Missing labels, had there been any, would have been
bounded over the full planned count rather than silently counted as denials.

The large-effect threshold, set before the run, is 0.30. If the interval's
upper limit falls below it, the large effect is ruled out for this public
edit method; if its lower limit is at least 0.30, the data support a large
effect; otherwise the comparison is inconclusive. The rule tests a pre-set
minimum effect worth detecting; it is not an equivalence test and does not
show that the effect is zero. Specificity, the target minus the average of
the control sets, uses simultaneous limits over all eight condition
probabilities, with a tail of $0.05/16$ for each. Every control set, both
rubrics and the untreated condition are reported.

\subsection{Main steering result}
\label{sec:ensemble-results}

All 450 trials completed, with 50 complete blocks and no missing labels or
empty turns. Of the 900 turns, \SteeringTruncContinuationCapped{} first turns
hit the length limit, spread across all nine conditions
(\SteeringTruncContinuationArmLow{} to \SteeringTruncContinuationArmHigh{}
each), and so did \SteeringTruncAnswerCapped{} of the scored answers:
\SteeringTruncTargetSuppressionCapped{} under target suppression,
\SteeringTruncTargetAmplificationCapped{} under target amplification,
\SteeringTruncZeroCapped{} untreated and \SteeringTruncControlCapped{} in the
control sets. All stay in the analysis. Without steering,
$\EnsemblePaperZeroPositive{}/\EnsemblePaperZeroValid{}$ answers are labeled
positive under the paper rubric and
$\EnsembleNotebookZeroPositive{}/\EnsembleNotebookZeroValid{}$ under the
second rubric, on the same outputs.

Steering the target features gives
$\EnsemblePaperTargetSuppressionPositive{}/\EnsemblePaperTargetSuppressionValid{}$
positive labels under suppression and
$\EnsemblePaperTargetAmplificationPositive{}/\EnsemblePaperTargetAmplificationValid{}$
under amplification. The difference is $\EnsemblePaperTargetGap{}$, with
conservative interval
$[\EnsemblePaperTargetCPLow{},\EnsemblePaperTargetCPHigh{}]$, whose upper
limit is below the +0.30 threshold. That threshold, kept from our first
public-weight protocol, was chosen there as a large shift that is still less
than half the source's 0.80, so the test asks for much less than the
published size. Read against other sizes, the upper limit also excludes +0.20
and the source's 0.80, but not +0.10. The paired-block bootstrap interval is
$[-0.14,0.06]$, a secondary summary that does not enter the decision. Under
the second rubric the difference is $\EnsembleNotebookTargetGap{}$, with
conservative interval
$[\EnsembleNotebookTargetCPLow{},\EnsembleNotebookTargetCPHigh{}]$, which
excludes the source's 0.80 but not +0.30, so that comparison is
inconclusive; its paired-bootstrap interval is $[-0.20,0.16]$.

Relabeling the capped final answers does not recover a large effect. In a
post-hoc check, we treated the
\SteeringTruncTargetCapped{} capped target answers, all labeled positive under
the paper rubric, as unknown and recomputed the conservative interval so that
it holds for every possible label they could have received. Under the paper
rubric it widens to $[\SteeringTruncPaperLow{},\SteeringTruncPaperHigh{}]$,
which still excludes +0.30; under the second rubric its upper limit becomes
$\SteeringTruncNotebookHigh{}$, which still excludes the source's 0.80. This
does not test what longer inductions or answers would have produced.

How hard did this push the model? The median edit was
\SteeringDoseEditMedianPct\% of the size of the model's internal state at the
edited layer (10th to 90th percentile across non-special-token positions
\SteeringDoseEditLowPct--\SteeringDoseEditHighPct\%) and arrived as requested
at every such measured position (cosine with the requested edit at least
\SteeringDoseCosineMin). It was large enough to change the text: compared
with the untreated run that used the same random seed, the first turn
differed in \SteeringDoseTextChangedLow\% to \SteeringDoseTextChangedHigh\% of
target runs. Yet those runs kept the untreated run's paper-rubric label in
\SteeringDoseLabelSameLow\% to \SteeringDoseLabelSameHigh\% of cases. Measured
against the features themselves, the push was gentle. A weight of 0.5 is only
\SteeringDosePeakShareLow{} to \SteeringDosePeakShareHigh{} of the mapping-corpus
reference activation (the typical maximum in designed feature-matching texts) of
the three features whose activation maps best match their labels, 41533, 58667
and 30686, although it exceeds that reference for the two weakest, 23893 and
22004. These scales were measured with NF4 model weights, not the BF16 weights
used here, and are not estimates of the natural activation distribution.
Well-known steering demonstrations clamp a feature to five or ten times its
maximum activation \citep{templeton2024scaling}; by that standard, this test
pushed the best-matched features lightly. It therefore rules out a large
effect at this dose, not at every dose; Section~\ref{sec:operator-matching} reports stronger doses for two of
the features.

Under the paper rubric, the three control sets give differences of
$\EnsemblePaperControlOneGap{}$, $\EnsemblePaperControlTwoGap{}$ and
$\EnsemblePaperControlThreeGap{}$; the target minus their average is
$\EnsemblePaperTargetMinusControls{}$, with simultaneous interval
$[\EnsemblePaperTargetMinusControlsCPLow{},\EnsemblePaperTargetMinusControlsCPHigh{}]$.
Under the second rubric, the control differences are
$\EnsembleNotebookControlOneGap{}$, $\EnsembleNotebookControlTwoGap{}$ and
$\EnsembleNotebookControlThreeGap{}$, and the target minus their average is
$\EnsembleNotebookTargetMinusControls{}$
$[\EnsembleNotebookTargetMinusControlsCPLow{},\EnsembleNotebookTargetMinusControlsCPHigh{}]$.
Neither interval excludes zero, so we cannot say whether the target features
behave differently from the controls.
