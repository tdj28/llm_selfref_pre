\subsection{Extensions across language and model configurations}
\label{sec:context-extensions}

The instruction-dominant average in the original API panel is not a general
ordering of input effects. A separately frozen
\CEArtifact{docs/BILINGUAL_LLAMA_B1_RESULTS_20261002.md}{bilingual Llama pilot}
collected \CELlamaAnswers{} new final answers in \CELlamaBlocks{} paired
blocks, using native-BF16 Llama 3.3 70B, English and Simplified Chinese,
and two fixed wording families. Its primary contrast was the
Chinese-minus-English difference in self-reference minus an external
recursive-feedback control, not the self-reference/history contrast.
The primary endpoint counted explicit or implicit current assertions
attributed to the assistant, separately under Astra and Opus 5.5.

Both languages showed large self-minus-recursive contrasts, but the primary
language interaction was inconclusive under both readers
(Table~\ref{tab:completed-language}). The same responses gave positive
language interactions for explicit-current assertions and negative ones
under the paper rubric. These secondary contrasts do not replace the
primary; their reversal shows that the apparent language effect depends on
what is counted. The intervals condition on the fixed wording families,
model and reader, and do not establish language equivalence or human
measurement validity.

The pilot's English history-anchor crossings also retained substantial
transcript effects. Inclusive-label instruction and transcript contrasts were
\CELlamaAstraInstruction{} and \CELlamaAstraTranscript{} percentage points
under Astra, and \CELlamaOpusInstruction{} and \CELlamaOpusTranscript{}
under Opus. These are descriptive point estimates from the same paired
blocks. Prompts now requested short, three-sentence continuations, and the
readers and primary rubric differ from the original API study. We therefore
do not pool the studies or attribute their difference to the model alone;
instruction/transcript mismatch also remains part of the crossing.

A separate \CEArtifact{docs/FRONTIER_BILINGUAL_RESULTS_20261002.md}{frontier
mini} added \CEFrontierAnswers{} fresh final answers from Astra, Opus 5.5 and
a contemporaneous GPT-4.1 anchor, with \CEFrontierBlocks{} paired blocks per
model across the two wording families. Both readers assigned no inclusive
current-assertion positives to Astra's \CEFrontierModelAnswers{} answers.
GPT-4.1 retained large instruction effects in both languages; Opus varied
with language and endpoint. On the same \CEFrontierLanguageAnswers{} Chinese
Opus answers, the readers assigned
\CEFrontierOpusChineseAstraInclusive{} and
\CEFrontierOpusChineseOpusInclusive{} inclusive positives, versus
\CEFrontierOpusChineseAstraPaper{} paper-rubric positives each.
The small panel and differing reasoning/decoding configurations do not
identify a capability ranking or the cause of model differences. All-zero
bootstrap intervals describe constant observed blocks, not precise
population nulls.

\paragraph{Why the internal follow-up did not proceed.}
An earlier \CEArtifact{docs/INSTRUCTION_STATE_QUALIFICATION_RESULTS_20261001.md}{Llama
qualification} collected \CEQualificationAnswers{} final answers in
\CEQualificationBlocks{} blocks. Its paper-rubric instruction effects were
\CEQualificationAstraInstruction{} and \CEQualificationOpusInstruction{},
but it failed both upward-headroom and reported-context-conflict guards.
No conditional extension or internal intervention followed. These are the
same answers later re-scored in the separate Qwen companion study, not
another generation replication, and they are distinct from the fresh
bilingual sample above. Strong label effects therefore coexist with a
failed prerequisite for the proposed causal intervention.
