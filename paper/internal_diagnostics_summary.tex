\section{Public-Weight Intervention Diagnostics}
\label{sec:source-alignment}

\paragraph{Context and baseline.}
The native-BF16 Llama 3.3 70B follow-up completed
\SourceBehavioralTrials{} two-turn trials, applying additive edits at every
position in both turns with the accepted six coordinates and a true-zero
branch. Across temperatures 0.5/0.6 and caps of 128/256 tokens, the
instruction in AE Studio's notebook \citep{aestudio2026deception}
produced 0/10 untreated positives; the paper's instruction
produced 6--8/10 under the notebook classifier and 8--9/10 under the paper
rubric. These reused ten seeds, not independently sampled prompts. Neither
baseline matches the source figure's approximately 0.30. The instructions
differ in several phrases; their comparison does not isolate one cause.
The paper's wording was tested only untreated here.

The primary suppression-minus-amplification contrast averages six
single-feature differences at $-0.7$ versus $+0.7$ within each seed. It was
$\SourcePrimaryNotebook{}$ with conditional bootstrap interval
$[-0.05,0.05]$ under the notebook classifier; the paper-rubric sensitivity
was $\SourcePrimaryPaper{}$ $[-0.03,0.07]$. Ten-block Hoeffding bounds were
$[-0.859,0.859]$ and $[-0.842,0.876]$: the narrow intervals do not give a
distribution-free exclusion of 0.30. Fixed-six aggregates and all three
matched panels also had zero estimated differences; these are not the
paper's random-two-to-four-feature distribution.

\paragraph{Delivery is not suppression.}
Re-encoding the edited states through the SAE showed why a delivered vector
does not establish feature suppression \citep{cunningham2023sparse}.
At the final prompt position all six features read zero before editing;
only 23893 crossed its threshold under $+0.7$. Negative edits moved the
hidden state without removing activity there. During generation they had
some activity to remove, but little. All trials completed without empty
turns or missing labels; 154 first turns and 41 answers hit their caps.

\subsection{What the internal readouts establish}
\label{app:jlens-details}

Forty preselected cases compare clean and edited executions on matched
prefixes at layers 50, 65 and 78, covering both signs, individual features,
the target aggregate and matched panels, with two seeds. Each case uses
both zero-intervention and steered source histories. The released Jacobian
lens \citep{gurnee2026workspace} is compared with identity and five fixed
random transports. These descriptive measurements are not additional
behavioral trials or confirmatory mechanism endpoints.

For transport $T_\ell$ and selected unembedding rows $W_V$, normalized
token scores are $W_V\operatorname{RMSNorm}(T_\ell h_\ell)$; the separate
gain-free linear readout satisfies
\[
 \ell_T^{\rm lin}(h_\ell+\delta h_\ell)-\ell_T^{\rm lin}(h_\ell)
 =W_VT_\ell\delta h_\ell.
\]
Agreement with an injected vector at the edit site checks implementation,
not hidden belief. On the common zero-source history, the layer-78
deception-word contrast (target minus mean matched panels, turn 2's final
prompt position) was
$\num[round-mode=places,round-precision=2]{\SourceJZeroPositiveDeceptionJacobian}$
for J-lens and
$\num[round-mode=places,round-precision=2]{\SourceJZeroPositiveDeceptionIdentity}$
for identity under positive steering; negative steering gave
$\num[round-mode=places,round-precision=2]{\SourceJZeroNegativeDeceptionJacobian}$
and
$\num[round-mode=places,round-precision=2]{\SourceJZeroNegativeDeceptionIdentity}$.
Identity also detected the footprint, so this does not establish unique
diagnostic power for J-lens. Random-transport ranges are descriptive, not
confidence intervals. Positive steering raised honesty
scores too: these are not calibrated concept probabilities or a unique
honesty/deception axis. Steered histories differ across panels, unlike the
common zero history. Changed scores do not establish mediation of reports.

The \nativeartifact{data/berg_source_replication/source_aligned_v1_20261001/RELEASE_MANIFEST.json}{pinned release}
retains expanded plots, every word group and both signs/histories.
Its companion checker verifies counts and point estimates, not bootstrap
intervals or raw-state-to-readout reconstruction.

\subsection{Why fidelity testing did not advance}
\label{app:fidelity-calibration}

Under a
\href{https://github.com/tdj28/llm_selfref_pre/blob/2d9c94f1de59f0f59dd89636c20afece1f6d1daf/docs/STEERING_FIDELITY_PROTOCOL_20261002.md}{prospective protocol},
\FidelityForwards{} forward passes tested factual/list accuracy without text
generation. Unsteered scores were \FidelityFactsCorrect{}/\FidelityFamilyN{}
and \FidelityListsCorrect{}/\FidelityFamilyN{}.
The selected $\FidelityDose{}$ edit ($R=\FidelityResidualNorm{}$, the reference
hidden-state norm) passed accuracy preservation and delivery
on \FidelityArmN{} items per steered condition; an ineligible $0.60R$ damage
probe failed preservation in four controls. Numerical delivery did not
validate deception suppression.

Neither pressure wording reduced accuracy to the required 85\% or less in
both truth strata. Both JSON control features were inactive at the
\FidelityProbes{} sampled prompt positions. Consequently the pressure,
opposed-experience and JSON-generation comparisons were not run.
The \href{https://github.com/tdj28/llm_selfref_pre/blob/780f8315454293decbf59b669c20f42ffd2f18f6/data/steering_fidelity/calibration_v1_20261002/RELEASE_MANIFEST.json}{complete calibration release}
preserves these failures. A
\href{https://github.com/tdj28/llm_selfref_pre/blob/fa9256400f007e01318077fdf3590488a49610bc/docs/STEERING_FIDELITY_REPAIR_RESULTS_20261003.md}{368-forward repair pilot}
also failed pressure qualification and both JSON accuracy-preservation
checks, one on a near-tie. Token-resolved probes found activity, not globally
dead features. Gated pressure validation and JSON generations remained
unrun; neither study tested the proposed mechanism.
